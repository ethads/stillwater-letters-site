Daily Focus 📖 Full guide

Your five-minute daily check-in — ticks reset each morning
  • Small daily actions beat big monthly efforts. Marketing compounds like interest — it rewards showing up regularly, not occasionally.
  • If something on the list doesn't apply today, tick it anyway and move on. The goal is a rhythm, not perfection.
  • Engagement matters most. Replying to comments and DMs signals to platforms that your content is worth showing to more people — it's more powerful than posting more often.
  • Five minutes a day is enough. Don't let this become another source of pressure.

Growth Scoreboard 📖 Full guide

Update your numbers once a week — click any number to edit
  • Your email list is the most important number here. You own it — unlike social followers, no platform can take it away from you.
  • Pinterest is slow at first — monthly views grow over months, not days. A pin you create today might get most of its traffic in six months. Be patient.
  • For Instagram and Facebook, pay more attention to whether people are commenting and replying than to follower count. Engagement beats reach.
  • Update on Mondays. Five minutes. You're tracking trends, not obsessing over daily changes.

Content Pipeline 📖 Full guide

Plan what you're creating and track it from idea to live
  • Write once, use everywhere. One newsletter can become a Pinterest graphic, an Instagram caption, a Facebook post, and a blog excerpt. That's five pieces of content from one piece of writing.
  • Start with the newsletter — it's your most thoughtful, long-form piece. Break it into shorter pieces for social afterwards.
  • Status guide: Idea = just a thought. Draft = being written. Ready = finished, waiting to publish. Live = published.
  • Aim for 1 newsletter per week and 3–5 Pinterest pins. Everything else is a bonus, not a requirement.

Lead Funnel 📖 Full guide

The free offers that invite people to join your list
  • A lead magnet is a free gift — a PDF, prayer guide, devotional, or short email series — that someone receives in exchange for joining your list. It's your "thank you for signing up."
  • The best lead magnets solve one specific, named problem. "A morning ritual for overwhelmed women" converts better than "A wellness guide."
  • You don't need many — one or two strong offers work better than ten weak ones. Focus on depth, not breadth.
  • Track where your sign-ups come from — Pinterest? Instagram bio? Word of mouth? Knowing this helps you put your energy into what's already working.

Membership 📖 Full guide

Free subscribers, Hands of Hope members, and conversion rate
  • Most people need 7–10 touchpoints before they're ready to pay. Your newsletter is doing that work every single week — even when it feels like nothing is happening, it is.
  • A healthy conversion rate is 2–5%. If you have 500 free subscribers, 10–25 paid members is strong. Don't compare yourself to anyone else.
  • The best time to invite someone to Hands of Hope is right after they've replied to your newsletter, shared something, or reached out personally. Warmth converts. Broadcasts don't.
  • Watch the conversion rate trend over months — a slow, steady rise is exactly what success looks like here.

Testimonials 📖 Full guide

Log what readers say — your most powerful marketing tool
  • Other people's words are ten times more convincing than yours. A reader saying "this newsletter changed my morning" sells Stillwater better than any description you could write.
  • Capture testimonials the moment they arrive — from email replies, DMs, comments, and conversations. They're easy to lose if you don't log them immediately.
  • Use them on your sign-up page, in social posts, in your welcome emails, and especially when inviting people to Hands of Hope.
  • A simple way to ask: "Your message meant so much to me. Would it be okay if I shared it with others? I can use just your first name." Almost everyone says yes.
