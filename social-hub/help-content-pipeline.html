💡 Why you need this

🔴 CriticalNo content plan = no consistent growth

The #1 reason newsletters stall is that the creator runs out of ideas mid-week and skips a send. A pipeline means you always have your next two or three topics waiting. Missing one week of email is far more damaging than it sounds — open rates drop, readers disengage, and the algorithm on Pinterest stops favouring your pins.

🟡 ImportantOne piece of writing can become five pieces of content

Most creators don't realise this. Your newsletter becomes a Pinterest graphic caption, an Instagram post, a Facebook update, and a blog paragraph. This guide shows you exactly how to do that without it feeling like extra work.

🟢 GoalOne newsletter per week, 3–5 pins, and the rest flows naturally

That's the target. Once you have a pipeline habit, this takes less than 90 minutes a week total. Start smaller — even one piece of content per week puts you ahead of most people.

👣 Step-by-step walkthrough

Follow these steps to add, manage, and move content through your pipeline.

Click "+ Add Content" in your Command Centre

This button is in the top-right corner of the Content Pipeline section. A form will slide open.

💡 You can add content from your phone while you're on the go. If an idea comes to you during your morning walk, open the hub and capture it before you forget.

Enter a topic or working title

This doesn't have to be your final newsletter subject line. It's just a label so you know what the piece is about. Examples that work well:

  • "Worry and prayer — Phil 4:6"
  • "When rest feels selfish"
  • "What I've learned about waiting on God"
  • "5 things that help me on hard mornings"

Choose the content Type

Pick what kind of content this is. If one newsletter will turn into multiple pieces, add them as separate rows (one for the newsletter, one for the Pinterest pin, etc.).

Set the Status — this is the most important field

The status tells you (and the pipeline) where this piece is in its journey. Update it as you go.

IDEA Just a thought — not started yet
DRAFT You've started writing it
READY Finished — waiting to be sent or posted
LIVE Published and out in the world 🎉
💡 Change the status directly in the table — click the coloured badge in the Status column and choose the new status. You don't need to re-open the form.

Add a date and click Save

The date is when you plan to publish or post it — not when you added the idea. Even a rough target ("second week of July") is helpful. Use the Monday of that week as the date.

♻️ The repurposing method — write once, share everywhere

This is how experienced creators produce a lot of content without burning out.

Start with your newsletter… Then create this Extra effort Why it's worth it
One newsletter about morning prayer routines Pinterest graphic — pull the main quote or tip, make a vertical image with Canva
Pinterest drives traffic for months
Instagram caption — use the opening paragraph of the newsletter
Reaches people who prefer visual feeds
Facebook post — ask a question based on the newsletter theme ("Do you have a morning ritual?")
Sparks conversation and visibility
Second Pinterest pin — use a different quote from the same newsletter
More coverage from same content
  1. Go to canva.com and sign up for a free account if you don't have one.
  2. In the search bar, type "Pinterest Pin" and choose any template you like.
  3. Replace the template text with your quote or tip from the newsletter.
  4. Change the colours to match Stillwater's feel — sage green (#3D5A3E), cream (#FAF7F2), and gold (#B8924A).
  5. Download as a PNG and upload it to Pinterest with your caption.

Canva's free plan is plenty for this. You don't need the paid version.

  • Start with a keyword — "Morning prayer routine for Christian women who feel overwhelmed"
  • Write 2–3 sentences in a warm, personal tone — the same voice you use in your newsletter.
  • End with a gentle call to action: "Read the full letter at The Stillwater Letters."
  • Add your website link in the destination URL so clicks go straight to your sign-up page.
  • Use hashtags sparingly — 3 to 5 is plenty. More than that looks spammy.

📖 Want to go deeper?

Expand any topic you'd like to understand better.

Once a month, spend 20 minutes on this:

  1. Open your Command Centre and go to Content Pipeline.
  2. Add 4 newsletter ideas — one per week for the coming month. They can be rough titles.
  3. For each newsletter, add one Pinterest pin idea (same topic, different angle).
  4. That's 8 rows in your pipeline — you now have a full month mapped out.
  5. Each week, move that week's newsletter from "Idea" to "Draft" and write it.

This approach removes the weekly "what do I write?" panic and replaces it with simply executing a plan you've already made.

  • Your inbox: What questions have readers asked you this week? Each question is a newsletter topic.
  • Your quiet time: What scripture or thought stayed with you this week? Write about that.
  • Seasons of life: What are women in your audience experiencing right now — school holidays, end of year, winter, busy season? Meet them where they are.
  • Old letters: Look at your best-performing newsletters (highest open rates). Write a follow-up or a related piece.
  • Pinterest search: Type "faith" or "prayer" into Pinterest's search bar and look at what topics come up. The suggestions show what people are actively searching for.

Aim for a "swipe file" — a note on your phone where you capture ideas as they come to you throughout the week. When you sit down to plan, you'll have a list waiting.

The number that matters most: email open rate.

  • Below 20% — something's off. Subject lines may need work, or you may be sending too infrequently.
  • 20–35% — solid and normal for a newsletter of your size.
  • Above 35% — excellent. Your audience loves hearing from you.

For Pinterest: monthly views growing month-on-month is the signal to watch. It takes 3–6 months to build momentum — be patient and consistent.

For Instagram: comments and saves matter more than likes. A post with 5 comments beats one with 100 likes — it means people are engaged.
