💡 Why these four numbers?

🔴 Most importantYour email list is the only number you truly own

Instagram can change its algorithm. Pinterest can remove your account. Facebook can restrict your reach overnight. But your email list belongs to you — no platform can take it away. Every subscriber is a direct line to a real person who chose to hear from you. Grow this number above everything else.

🟡 Second priorityPinterest monthly views are a long game — be patient

Pinterest is a search engine, not a social platform. A pin you create today may get most of its traffic in 6 months. Monthly views will seem low at first — that is normal. What matters is the trend: are they growing month on month? Even slowly growing is a sign the strategy is working.

🟢 Context metricsInstagram followers and Facebook likes tell part of the story

These numbers matter, but they're not your primary goal. A small, engaged Instagram audience that replies and saves your posts is far more valuable than a large, passive one. Watch these numbers for trends, but don't chase them at the expense of your email list.

Quick win — 5 minutes

🗺️ Where to find each number

Here's exactly where to look for each metric — no guessing required.

Your four scoreboard metrics — what they mean and where to find them
✉️ Email Subscribers Most important

Where to find it: Log into MailerLite → Dashboard → your subscriber count is at the top. Or go to Subscribers → All Subscribers and look at the total.

What's healthy: Growing by even 5–10 new subscribers per week is solid momentum for a newsletter at this stage.

📌 Pinterest Monthly Views Second priority

Where to find it: Log into Pinterest → click your profile picture (top right) → your monthly views are shown on your profile page just below your name.

What's healthy: Under 1,000/month in the first 3 months is normal. Growth to 5,000–20,000+ monthly views happens between months 4–12 with consistent pinning.

📷 Instagram Followers Context metric

Where to find it: Open Instagram → tap your profile → follower count is shown at the top of your profile page.

What matters more than followers: saves, shares, and replies to your posts. Watch those in your Instagram Insights (tap the bar chart icon on any post).

👍 Facebook Page Likes Context metric

Where to find it: Go to your Facebook Page → the Like count is shown on your page header. Or go to Page Insights → Overview for more detail.

What matters more than likes: post reach and comments. A post that reaches 500 people and gets 10 comments is more valuable than one that gets 50 likes.

1
Update all four numbers every Monday. Set a recurring reminder on your phone if that helps. The habit matters more than perfect accuracy.
2
Click any number to edit it. The number field in each scoreboard card is directly editable — just click it, type the new value, and hit Save Numbers.

👣 Step-by-step: updating your scoreboard

Open each platform in a new tab

On Mondays, open MailerLite, Pinterest, Instagram, and your Facebook Page in four browser tabs. This takes about 30 seconds and means all your numbers are in front of you at once.

Find each number and note any change from last week

You don't need to write this down anywhere else — just notice it. "Email went from 312 to 319 — that's 7 new subscribers." That brief moment of awareness is valuable.

💡 If a number went down, don't panic. Unsubscribes are normal — people's lives change. Focus on the overall trend over months, not week-to-week dips.

Click each number in the Scoreboard and update it

Click the large number in the card, type the new value, and move to the next one. When all four are updated, click "Save Numbers."

Ask yourself one question: "What's working?"

Did your email list grow? What did you send or share that week? Did Pinterest views jump? What did you pin? Connecting actions to results — even loosely — helps you do more of what works.

💡 If nothing grew this week, ask "Did I show up consistently?" If yes, keep going — results are often delayed. If no, that's your answer.

📖 Want to go deeper?

Open rate = the percentage of subscribers who open each newsletter you send. Find it in MailerLite under Campaigns → your sent campaign → Stats.

  • Below 20% — below average. Check your subject lines and make sure you're sending consistently.
  • 20–30% — solid for most newsletters. You're in a healthy range.
  • 30–50%+ — excellent. Your audience is engaged and wants to hear from you.

New newsletters often see higher open rates early (curious subscribers) that settle over time. This is normal. Consistency keeps it stable.

Pinterest is a search engine, not a social feed. When someone searches "morning prayer routine," Pinterest shows them pins that have historically been clicked and saved — not pins posted yesterday.

This means:

  • Pins you create today may not reach their peak traffic for 3–9 months.
  • Older, well-performing pins keep driving traffic indefinitely.
  • Consistency in the first 6 months is what determines your Pinterest future.

The mindset shift: Pinterest rewards patience. Every pin you make is an investment, not an immediate return. Keep going even when the numbers seem low.

Order of focus for The Stillwater Letters:

  1. Email list growth — this is your business foundation. Every other platform feeds into this.
  2. Pinterest — the best long-term driver of new email subscribers for faith and lifestyle content. Slow to build, but compound growth.
  3. Instagram — good for community and warmth. Less effective for list growth unless you have a clear call-to-action in your bio.
  4. Facebook — good for sharing newsletters and building a local community. Don't stress about growing this organically.

Don't try to master all four at once. Start with email + Pinterest for 3 months before adding Instagram focus.
