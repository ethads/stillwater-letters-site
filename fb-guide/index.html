Your page currently has around 20 followers and your first post is live. This guide covers everything proven to work for growing a small, values-led ministry page organically — roughly in the order you should tackle it.

No paid ads required to reach 200 followers. The goal is real women who want to be there, not inflated numbers.

Phase 1 — This week

Warm outreach & personal invites

Do first

Personal invites to every Facebook friend

Don't use the mass "Invite friends" button — it sends a generic notification that gets ignored. Instead, go through your friends list and send a short personal message to any woman you think this is for:

  • "Hey [name], I started a little ministry page called Stillwater — it's a free weekly prayer letter for women. Thought of you. Would love for you to follow it."
  • Aim for 30–50 personal messages this week
  • You'll probably convert 50–70% of people you personally message
Time: 1–2 hrs total · Cost: free

Do first

Ask 2–3 church leaders to share the page

Think of pastors, women's group leaders, or ministry friends in Adelaide who have their own Facebook following. Ask them personally (not via mass message) to share the page to their page or profile. One share from a trusted leader can bring 20–50 new followers in a day.

  • Give them exact wording so it's easy: "I'm sharing my friend Alice's new ministry page — she sends a free weekly prayer letter every Tuesday and it's the real thing. Go follow her."
Time: 30 mins · Cost: free (relationship capital)
This week

Pin the welcome post

Go to the welcome post you just published → click the three dots (⋯) → "Pin to top of Page". This means every new visitor sees it first. It should stay pinned permanently.

Time: 2 mins · Cost: free
This week

Use "Invite to Like" after every post gets reactions

After each post, click on the reaction count to see who reacted. Anyone who reacted but doesn't follow the page will have an "Invite" button next to their name. Click every one. Facebook allows this for free and it converts well — people already showed they liked the content.

Time: 5 mins after each post · Cost: free
Phase 2 — Weeks 2–4

Weekly posting rhythm

The one thing that matters most: show up every week, without fail.

The Facebook algorithm rewards pages that post consistently. Pages that post 3–4 times per week see significantly higher organic reach than pages that post occasionally. Most pages that give up do so in the first 6 weeks — push through that window and the momentum becomes self-sustaining.

Tuesday
Share the letter. Paste a meaningful excerpt (3–5 sentences) from that week's letter as the post text, then add: "The full letter is in your inbox — or sign up at thestillwaterletters.com.au". Include an image if you have one.
Thursday
Prayer prompt or short reflection. A single question or one-paragraph thought. Something like: "What are you carrying into the weekend that you haven't handed over yet?" No image needed — text posts work well for engagement.
Saturday
Scripture graphic or personal moment. A verse that's been meaningful this week, with a one-line personal note from Alice. Or a photo of something ordinary — morning coffee, journal, a quiet moment — with a short reflection.
Best posting times for Australian audiences: Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday mornings between 7–9am ACST. People check Facebook over breakfast before the day starts.
Phase 3 — Ongoing

Content ideas that drive shares

Shares are the single fastest organic growth lever on Facebook. When someone shares your post, it reaches all their friends who don't follow you. Design some posts specifically to be shareable.

Highly shareable

Relatable "me too" posts. A one-sentence observation about the ordinary struggle of faith. "The weeks I most need the letter are the weeks I almost don't open it." Women tag friends in these.

Highly shareable

Scripture + short personal note. Not a graphic — just the verse and two sentences from Alice. Authentic beats polished every time. People share these to their own walls.

Good reach

Questions that invite answers. "What's one thing you're praying for this week that you haven't told anyone?" People comment, which tells the algorithm the post matters, which extends reach.

Good reach

Reader responses (anonymised). "A reader wrote to me this week and said something that stopped me..." Share the essence (with permission or anonymised). These feel personal and get reshared.

Seasonal

Honest faith moments. "I haven't had a proper quiet time in three weeks and I'm a minister. Here's what I did instead." Vulnerability is Alice's superpower — use it.

Seasonal

"Send this to a woman who needs it." End posts occasionally with this line. Direct asks to share work better than hoping people will.

Phase 4 — Biggest untapped reach

Facebook Reels — the algorithm's free gift

Reels reach people who don't follow you. Nothing else does.

Standard posts reach mostly your existing followers (and only a fraction of them). Reels get distributed to non-followers based on topic and watch behaviour — they're Facebook's answer to TikTok. A single Reel from a page with 20 followers can reach 2,000 people if it's watchable. This is the single highest-leverage free tool available.

High leverage

Reels for the camera-shy: text-on-screen style

Alice doesn't need to appear on camera. The most effective low-effort Reel format for this content is: a short clip of still water, morning light, or hands holding a Bible, with text overlaid as words fade in. Add quiet ambient music. 30–60 seconds.

  • Use a free app like CapCut (on iPhone) — drag in a clip, add text, add music
  • Content: one paragraph from the weekly letter, broken into short phrases that appear one at a time
  • End with: "Full letter every Tuesday — link in bio"
  • Even one Reel per week is transformative for reach
Time: 20–30 mins once you have a template · Cost: free
Good option

Audio-only style Reel

If Alice is comfortable with her voice but not her face: record a 45-second audio reading of a letter excerpt (voice memo on iPhone), pair it with a static image of still water, upload as a Reel. Many faith creators do this — it feels intimate and personal without requiring video presence.

Time: 15 mins · Cost: free
Phase 5 — Community leverage

Facebook Groups strategy

High leverage

Join existing Australian Christian women's groups

Search Facebook for: "Christian women Australia", "women of faith Australia", "prayer warriors women", "Christian mums Adelaide/South Australia". Join as Alice personally (not as the page). Participate genuinely for 2–3 weeks — answer questions, share encouragement, pray for people. Then when it's natural, mention the page or letter.

  • Never drop the link cold — build trust first
  • A warm mention in a comment ("I write about exactly this in my weekly letter…") is far more effective than a promotional post
  • Target 3–5 active groups
Time: 20 mins/week · Cost: free
Consider later

Create a Stillwater private Facebook Group

Once the page reaches 100–200 followers, create a private group called something like "The Stillwater Circle" — a place for readers to share prayer requests, respond to letters, and build fellowship. This creates a second growth engine: members invite friends to the group, group members follow the page.

  • Private groups feel safer and more intimate than public pages — retention is much higher
  • Don't launch it until you have enough followers to seed it with 20–30 active members or it'll feel empty
  • Connected to your Prayer Circle concept already on the website
Time: 1 hr to set up, 20 mins/week to manage · Cost: free

Things to avoid

What doesn't work

Avoid

Mass "Invite Friends" button

Sends a generic notification that gets dismissed. Personal messages convert at 5–10× higher rate. Worth the extra effort.

Avoid

Posting into groups as the page to promote

Most groups block promotional posts, and cold drops of a link look spammy. Participate as a person, not as a marketer.

Avoid

Buying followers or likes

Fake followers tank your engagement rate, which tells the algorithm your content isn't worth showing to real people. It actively makes growth harder.

Avoid

Inconsistency

Posting 5 times one week then nothing for 2 weeks is worse than posting 3 times a week every week. The algorithm deprioritises pages that go quiet.
